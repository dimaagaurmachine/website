How it runs in your school

This is the whole process. There are no variations.

  1. 1

    You approve the batch

    Management reviews and sanctions the program. The school provides a venue and a time slot, and informs parents. Enrolment is each family's choice.

  2. 2

    We run it in school hours

    Two or three days, a maximum of four hours a day. One of your teachers is present in every session, and students never use AI tools unsupervised. Taught in a mix of English and Hindi.

  3. 3

    It ends

    Every student receives a school-endorsed participation certificate. The program is one-time: no subscription, no upsell, and no follow-up program to decline later.

For parents

Schools usually forward this part to families. These are the questions that actually come up — including the ones nobody asks out loud.

Everything happens inside the school building, during school hours, with one of the school's own teachers present in every session. Students never use AI tools unsupervised, and there is no part of the program that happens at home or online after hours.

This is the most common concern, and it is a fair one. Students today already use these tools — usually with nobody explaining the limits. The program treats AI as a thinking assistant, and a substantial part of it is about when not to use it: how to spot a wrong or invented answer, why copying output defeats the point, and what misuse actually costs them.

No. It is two or three days in total, scheduled by the school inside its own timetable, with a maximum of four hours a day. There is no homework and no after-school commitment. The program is supplementary — it does not replace exams, marks or board preparation, and it does not claim to.

The two-day program is ₹1,200–₹1,500 per student and the three-day program is up to ₹1,700. Parents pay this directly, and enrolment is voluntary — the school informs families, but taking part is each family's choice. It is a one-time fee. There is no subscription, no material to buy later, and nothing that gets sold to you afterwards.

Neither. Sessions run on a mix of school-provided devices and whatever students already have, depending on availability. And this is not a coding class — it is open to Science, Commerce and Arts students equally, and no programming background is assumed at any point.

Concretely: a school-endorsed participation certificate, and — on the three-day version — one complete working project they can demonstrate or reference later.

Less visibly, but more usefully: the ability to turn a chapter into their own flashcards and practice questions, the judgement to tell a good AI answer from a confidently wrong one, and a realistic picture of which careers this field actually contains and which subjects lead there.
