Why now

Students are becoming AI consumers.

They ask, copy, and trust the answer. Meanwhile the professions they are preparing for are changing faster than any syllabus. The World Economic Forum expects 170 million new jobs and 92 million displaced by 2030, with AI and big data the fastest-growing skills. The new jobs go to people who can build with AI, not just use it.

AI is now a life skill

Like reading a balance sheet or speaking in public, it now matters in medicine, law, design and business, not only in software.

Professions move faster than education

Students in Classes 8 to 12 choose subjects and colleges for careers that will look different by the time they graduate.

Blind trust is the real risk

Schools teach the ill effects of the internet. Very few teach where AI goes wrong, or where using it turns into misusing it.

The format

Two or three days. The school chooses.

Four hours a day, inside school hours. Not a lecture: every day runs in cycles of about 30 minutes of concept, in the AV room through games, stories and live demos, then 30 minutes of building in the computer lab. Teams work on one product from the first hour to the last, in front of the instructor and their teacher, and fix what breaks along the way.

2-day workshop · 8 hours
Learn, build, finish.

Concepts and building, one cycle at a time. By the end of day two, every team has a working AI product with a shareable link, shown in a demo round. Certificates for every student.

3-day workshop · 12 hours · Recommended
Everything in two days, then pitch.

Advanced modules applied to their product, then a 5-minute pitch per team, judged by a school nominee and the instructor, with awards for the Winner, the Runner-up and the Best Innovator. Pitches run in the AV room, with parents invited or as a larger school event.

Best on consecutive days: Thursday to Saturday for three days, Friday and Saturday for two, so students stay with their build. Saturday to Saturday is possible, but needs more revision time.

Where school AI education is heading

CBSE introduced Computational Thinking and AI for Classes 3–8 from the 2026–27 session, and CISCE offers Robotics and Artificial Intelligence as an ICSE subject in Classes 9–10. Schools that give students hands-on building experience now will be ahead of that shift, not catching up to it.

Not Computer Applications, not robotics

Computer Applications teaches programming for the board exam, and robotics teaches hardware. This is about AI itself, for every student, whatever they want to become. It complements both, and students who enjoy them often gain the most: AI is the layer that now sits on top of both.

FAQ

Questions principals ask

Which classes is it for? +

Classes 8 to 12, any stream. Classes 8–10 take one workshop. Classes 11–12 take a separate, more advanced workshop, and the two years can run together. No coding is needed in either.

Two days or three? +

The school chooses. Both run 4 hours a day inside school hours. The 2-day workshop ends with a finished AI agent per team. The 3-day workshop adds advanced modules and a judged 5-minute pitch per team, with prizes. Consecutive days work best: Thursday to Saturday, or Friday and Saturday.

How is this different from Computer Applications or robotics? +

Computer Applications teaches programming for the board exam, and robotics teaches hardware. This teaches AI itself, as a life skill: how it works, how to build with it, and where not to trust it. It sits alongside both and replaces neither.

What do we need to provide? +

Access to your computer lab and AV room, one or two teachers in the room, and 4 hours a day for 2 or 3 days. We bring headphones for every team if the lab does not have them, and everything students build on.

Is it safe? Whose accounts do students use? +

Students never sign up for anything or enter names, phone numbers or emails. All tools run in a workspace the instructor manages, and the AI services used are named to the school in advance. A student’s own voice is used only with written parental consent, and voice samples and conversation logs are deleted when the product goes offline. Misuse of AI, and where it begins, is part of what they learn. A school teacher is in the room throughout, and students are photographed only with written parental consent.

What does it cost, and who pays? +

Parents pay a one-time, all-inclusive fee per student through the school’s own fee system, in the range of a school robotics or activity workshop. Cancelling is free up to 7 days before the workshop. The exact fee and full terms are in your school’s written proposal. The student program costs the school nothing. The teacher program, Chalk aur Calibration, is quoted to the school separately.

Who teaches the sessions? +

Amaan Shaikh designs every session and teaches the in-school workshops himself. He is a Software Development Engineer building AI systems in Germany, with an M.Sc. in Computer Science from the University of Konstanz, and has taught 500+ students at Konstanz and MIT ADT University.
